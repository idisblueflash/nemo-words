# C-024 Syllable

**Meaning:** One beat of a word's US pronunciation: one vowel sound and the consonants that go with it. The `syllabify` step cuts the word's IPA into syllables and puts a dot at each cut. A consonant between two vowels goes to the later syllable when an English syllable can start with it.
**Example:** germane is /dʒɚ.ˈmeɪn/, two syllables. In /meɪn/ the onset is /m/, the nucleus is /eɪ/ and the coda is /n/. The nucleus and the coda together are the rime, /eɪn/.
**Not the same as:** a chunk, which is the piece that the anchor search works on. Today each chunk is exactly one syllable. US-050 would let them differ: vapor has the syllables /veɪ.pɚ/, but its chunks could be /veɪp/ (vape) and /pɚ/ (per).
