Where the true rate can be

    More words make the range narrower

    The curve keeps the measured rate and changes only the number of words. The dot is your number of words. Hit counts come from the rates in SP-029 and SP-064.

    How to use the range

    • 1Compare ranges, not rates. If the range includes a baseline, the words cannot show which one is better. SP-064 in-three (30% – 54%) includes the always-cell-1 rule (39%).
    • 2A rate on the go bar can come from luck. 28 of 62 (45%) meets the exact bar, but its range starts at 33%. To be sure that a pick beats the always-cell-1 rule, it needs 32 of 62 (52%).
    • 362 words see only large differences, about 15 points or more. A 6-point difference, for example 45% against 39%, needs several hundred words.
    • 4Small sets give wide ranges. On a tuning set of 20 words, 4 hits (20%) is 8% – 42%. For E01's kept count, 14 of 20 (70%) is 48% – 85%.
    • 5Near 0% the simple range fails. For 0 of 10 it gives 0% – 0%, so it says that we are fully sure. Wilson gives 0% – 28%.
