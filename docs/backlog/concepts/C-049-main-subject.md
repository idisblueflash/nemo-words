# C-049 Main subject

**Meaning:** The one person, animal or group of them that does a story's action, or the cause of the action (see below). A person who only receives the action is not the main subject. When the story names no person but has an action, the main subject is the person who does the action (the actor, C-051). When the people in a story only react to a cause, the cause is the main subject, even when it is an object, and the people are onlookers (C-052, C-054). The caption gives the main subject's view: front, three-quarter, side, back or from above. An object has views too. The view is one part of must-see clarity: a front or three-quarter view scores highest, a side view in between, and a back view or a view from above lowest.
**Example:** surgeon's story "Power surge! An OR goes dark; she operates by headlamp." has the main subject `surgeon`. In cell 1 she is seen in three-quarter view, in cell 8 from the side, and in cell 5 from above. In ventilator ("Nurse Venice returns later; the machine breathes for the newborn.") the main subject is the nurse, not the newborn, because the nurse acts. period's story shows only a pier and the sea, so it has no main subject. In appeal ("A peal of bells attracts a curious crowd.") the crowd only reacts, so the bells are the main subject.
**Not the same as:** the angle exception (C-042), which is about the camera angle of the whole story; the main-subject view is about where the main subject's face and body point in one cell.
